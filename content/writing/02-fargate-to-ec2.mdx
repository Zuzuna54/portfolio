We cut the compute bill on an AI pipeline by about 94%. It wasn't a clever model choice. It was
giving up on Fargate.

Fargate is excellent until your workload shapes diverge. Ours had three: model-inference workers
that wanted sustained CPU, a tiny ingestion service that idled most of the day, and FFmpeg jobs
that were pure compute and didn't care about architecture.

Paying Fargate's per-task premium across all three meant paying peak pricing for workloads that
had nothing in common.

We moved to ECS on EC2 with three capacity providers: compute-optimized instances for the AI
workers, micro instances for ingestion, and ARM64 for FFmpeg. Autoscaling ran on queue backlog
per task rather than CPU — queue depth is the honest signal for a queue-driven system; CPU lags it
by minutes.

The lesson isn't "Fargate is expensive." It's that one capacity strategy across three workload
shapes means you're overpaying on at least two of them.
