summary: One queue with a type field is simpler right up until one slow stage starves every other one.

The obvious design for a multi-stage LLM pipeline is one queue with a `type` field on the message.
It is simpler, and it is wrong for the same reason a single thread pool is wrong.

We ran seven queues, one per stage, each with its own dead-letter queue.

The reason is that the stages have genuinely different shapes. Transcription is long and I/O-bound.
Speaker identification is a cascade of cheap checks with one expensive vision call at the end.
Video compilation is pure CPU. If they share a queue, they share a retry policy, a concurrency
limit, and a scaling signal — and the slowest stage sets the pace for all of them.

Separate queues mean a backed-up stage backs up alone.

The dead-letter queues matter more than the queues. Bounded retries — three attempts, then the
message moves to its DLQ with a fourteen-day retention — mean a poison message stops costing money
within seconds instead of retrying until someone notices the bill.

Seven DLQs also means "what is broken" is answerable by looking at which one has depth, rather
than by grepping logs.
