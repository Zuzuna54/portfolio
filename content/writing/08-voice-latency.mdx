title: Where the latency actually goes in a sub-500ms voice pipeline

summary: Half a second is the line where people stop talking to a computer and start talking.
---

Under about half a second, people just talk. Over it, they start behaving like they're using a
computer — over-enunciating, waiting, no longer interrupting. The number isn't arbitrary; it's the
threshold where a system stops feeling conversational.

Getting under it is not one optimization. It's refusing, at every stage, to wait for a complete
result before starting the next one.

Speech-to-text streams partial transcripts rather than returning at end-of-utterance. The model
starts generating on the partial. Text-to-speech starts synthesizing on the first clause rather
than the finished response. Audio starts playing on the first chunk.

Nothing in that chain has a "done" step before the next stage begins. The end-to-end latency is
roughly the slowest single hop plus the time to first token, not the sum of four complete stages.

The part that surprised me is how much of the remaining budget is not inference. It's network
round-trips, buffer sizes chosen for throughput rather than latency, and the audio driver on the
device. We spent as long tuning buffers as tuning models.
