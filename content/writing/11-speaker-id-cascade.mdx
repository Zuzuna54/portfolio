title: A 7-stage speaker-identification cascade
date: "2026-09-22"

summary: Order the checks by confidence, not by cleverness, and the expensive one runs almost never.

Given a call recording, work out who said what. Diarization gives you "speaker 1" and "speaker 2".
Turning those into people is the actual problem.

We ran seven stages, first match wins, ordered by confidence:

Calendar attendees. Internal directory match. Vision analysis of sampled video frames. External
search for people outside the organization. Manual assignment. Contextual inference from the
conversation itself. Anonymous.

The ordering is the design. The calendar knows who was invited and costs nothing to check. Vision
analysis on video frames is accurate and expensive, so it sits behind two cheap checks that resolve
most cases first. In practice the expensive stage runs on a small minority of speakers.

Two details that mattered more than expected. The directory match requires name *and* company, not
name alone — otherwise two people called John Smith at different companies collapse into one, and
that error propagates into every downstream relationship. And every assignment records which stage
produced it, so an operator can see why a name was chosen and re-curate.

First match wins also makes it deterministic, which makes it replayable.
