title: Autoscaling on backlog-per-task instead of CPU
date: "2026-09-24"
status: scheduled
summary: Queue depth alone is the wrong signal and CPU is a lagging one. The honest unit is messages per worker.
---

Autoscaling a queue-driven system on CPU is scaling on a lagging indicator. By the time CPU rises,
the backlog already grew; by the time it falls, you already over-provisioned.

Autoscaling on raw queue depth is closer but still wrong. Fifty messages with ten workers is
healthy. Fifty messages with one worker is an incident. Depth alone can't tell them apart.

The honest unit is **backlog per task** — queue depth divided by running workers. We computed it
every minute in a small function, published it as a custom metric, and target-tracked it at five
messages per worker.

The interesting bug is scaling from zero. Target tracking fires on *greater than*, so a service
sitting at zero tasks with messages waiting computes a division by zero, or returns exactly the
target and never breaches. We returned `max(messages, target) + 0.1` whenever tasks were zero and
messages weren't — deliberately just over the line, so a sleeping service reliably wakes.

That fraction is the least elegant line in the system and it is the one that makes scale-to-zero
safe to run.
