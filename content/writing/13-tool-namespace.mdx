title: "24 tools in one function-calling registry: organizing a namespace"

summary: Past about a dozen tools, the model's problem stops being capability and starts being selection.
---

A function-calling assistant with a handful of tools is easy. At twenty-four, the failure mode
changes: the model stops failing at *using* a tool and starts failing at *choosing* one.

We organized ours into five categories — search, retrieval, list, detail, temporal — and the
categories were as much for the model as for us. Tools that overlap in purpose need names and
descriptions that make the difference obvious in one line, because one line is what the model reads.

Two things helped more than anything else.

Dispatching in parallel. Most useful questions need three or four lookups, and running them
sequentially is the difference between two seconds and eight. Tool calls settle together rather than
in series.

And a max-tool-calls fallback. Without a ceiling, a model that has chosen badly will keep choosing —
call, get an unhelpful result, call again. A bound turns an expensive loop into a degraded answer,
which is strictly better.

The lesson generalizes: past a dozen tools, tool descriptions are prompt engineering, not
documentation. They are the only context the model has at the moment it decides.
